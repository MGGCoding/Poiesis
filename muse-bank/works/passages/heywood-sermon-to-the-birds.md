# The Sermon to the Birds (The Little Flowers of St Francis, ch. XVI)
*The Little Flowers of St Francis (Fioretti), attributed to Ugolino Brunforte, translated by W. (William) Heywood, Fioretti compiled late 14th century from the Latin Actus (c. 1327–1340); Heywood translation 1906* · Chapter XVI, the sermon itself (from the opening quotation mark) and the first part of the following sentence (ends mid-sentence, marked …)

```text
"My sisters the birds, much are ye beholden unto God your creator, and alway and in every place ought ye to praise Him, because He hath given you liberty to fly wheresoever ye will, and hath clothed you on with twofold and threefold raiment. Moreover, He preserved your seed in the ark of Noah that your race might not be destroyed. Again, ye are beholden unto Him for the element of the air which He hath appointed for you; furthermore, ye sow not neither do ye reap; yet God feedeth you and giveth you rivers and fountains wherefrom to drink; He giveth you mountains and valleys for your refuge, and high trees wherein to build your nests; and, in that ye know not how to sew nor spin, God clotheth you and your little ones; wherefore doth your Creator love you seeing that He giveth you so many benefits. Guard yourselves, therefore, my sisters the birds, from the sin of ingratitude and be ye ever mindful to give praise to God."

And, as St. Francis spake these words unto them, all those birds began to open their beaks, and to stretch out their necks, and to open their wings, and reverently to bow their heads even unto the ground …
```
