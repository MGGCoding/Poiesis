# Apology
*Plato, translated by Benjamin Jowett, c. 399–390 BC (original); Jowett translation 1871, 3rd ed. 1892* · Closing words of Socrates to the judges who voted to acquit him; one omission marked (the request about his sons)

```text
Wherefore, O judges, be of good cheer about death, and know of a certainty, that no evil can happen to a good man, either in life or after death. He and his are not neglected by the gods; nor has my own approaching end happened by mere chance. But I see clearly that the time had arrived when it was better for me to die and be released from trouble; wherefore the oracle gave no sign. For which reason, also, I am not angry with my condemners, or with my accusers; they have done me no harm, although they did not mean to do me any good; and for this I may gently blame them. … 

The hour of departure has arrived, and we go our ways—I to die, and you to live. Which is better God only knows.
```

## Proofing
Compared word by word and via sentence listing against Gutenberg #1656: IDENTICAL. The MIT Classics Jowett text (classics.mit.edu/Plato/apology.html) is an earlier Jowett version with different wording ('know this of a truth', 'to die and be released was better for me'); I followed Gutenberg consistently. author_died given as -347 (Plato d. 348/347 BC). 'better for me to die' answers Paul's 'to die is gain' closely. Gutenberg does not print the edition year; 1892 3rd ed. is my understanding of its basis, not verified on the page.
