# Don Quixote
*Miguel de Cervantes, translated by John Ormsby, 1615 (Part II); Ormsby translation 1885* · Part II, ch. 68, Sancho's whole reply on sleep

```text
"I don't know what that is," said Sancho; "all I know is that so long as I am asleep I have neither fear nor hope, trouble nor glory; and good luck betide him that invented sleep, the cloak that covers over all a man's thoughts, the food that removes hunger, the drink that drives away thirst, the fire that warms the cold, the cold that tempers the heat, and, to wind up with, the universal coin wherewith everything is bought, the weight and balance that makes the shepherd equal with the king and the fool with the wise man. Sleep, I have heard say, has only one fault, that it is like death; for between a sleeping man and a dead man there is very little difference."
```

## Proofing
Ormsby's wording differs from the brief: 'good luck betide him that invented sleep, the cloak that covers over all a man's thoughts' (the 'blessings on him… mantle…' wording is another translation). Verified on Gutenberg #5943 (states 'Translated by John Ormsby') by full compare (IDENTICAL) and targeted questions; one early check reported the opening as 'I don't understand that,' but two direct checks gave 'I don't know what that is,' — used the latter; the paragraph before ends '—post tenebras spero lucem.' and the reply after is 'Never have I heard thee speak so elegantly as now, Sancho'. The TAMU and publiconsulting copies are modern (copyright) translations — do not use. Ormsby 1829–1895 per Online Books Page. ~115 words.
